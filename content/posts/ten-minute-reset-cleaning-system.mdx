Most cleaning advice is written for a house that has already been cleaned. It assumes a free Saturday, a full cupboard of products, and the kind of energy that arrives only after you have finished everything else.

The ten-minute reset assumes the opposite. You are tired, it is 9 p.m., and the flat looks like a small weather event passed through it. The question is not "how do I clean the whole house?" but "what is the smallest set of actions that makes this feel like a home again?"

## Why cleaning systems fail

Three reasons, and none of them are about laziness.

**They are too big.** "Clean the kitchen" is a project, and projects need a start. "Wipe the counter and empty the sink" is a task, and tasks get done.

**They are scheduled on the wrong day.** A weekly plan allocates one hour to the bathroom every Thursday. Miss two Thursdays and the plan is broken, so you abandon it. A daily reset has no backlog, because yesterday's ten minutes never carried forward.

**They start with the wrong surface.** People begin with floors, which look the worst. But floors make no difference to how a room feels. Horizontal surfaces at eye level do.

<Note title="The baseline principle">
A home does not need to be clean. It needs to never fall below a baseline you can restore in ten minutes. Everything else is maintenance.
</Note>

## The four surfaces

The reset touches four things, in this order, because this order produces the biggest visual change per minute spent.

1. **The kitchen counter.** Clear it, wipe it, put back only what lives there permanently.
2. **The sink.** Rinse, quick scrub, no dishes left in it. An empty sink is the single greatest psychological win available in domestic life.
3. **The table you eat at.** Clear, wipe, one object allowed.
4. **The bathroom basin.** Toothpaste, hair, tap. Forty seconds.

Everything else — the sofa cushions, the shoes by the door, the pile of post — gets a single minute at the end, as triage rather than cleaning.

### The one-minute triage rule

Set a timer for one minute and return items to their rooms, not to their correct shelf. Perfection is what stops this step. A basket by the stairs that gets emptied weekly is better than a home where nothing is ever put away.

## The evening reset, minute by minute

| Minutes | Task |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 | Kitchen counter: clear, wipe, bin the scraps |
| 3–5 | Sink: rinse, scrub, dry the tap |
| 5–7 | Table: clear, wipe |
| 7–8 | Bathroom basin and mirror |
| 8–9 | One-minute triage: floors and doorways |
| 9–10 | Bin bag out, cloths rinsed, done |

That is the whole system. It is deliberately unambitious. If you finish early, stop — do not "just quickly" clean the cupboard, because that is how a ten-minute habit becomes an abandoned forty-minute project.

## The supporting rhythm

The reset handles the daily floor. Two smaller habits keep it stable.

**The morning exit sweep.** Three minutes before you leave: dishes in the rack away, bin checked, one thing taken out of the house. Returning to a reset home is a very different experience from returning to a wreck.

**The weekly fifteen.** Once a week, add one rotating job to the reset: vacuum, bathroom surfaces, sheets, fridge shelf, dusting. Fifteen minutes, one job, whatever the job is that week. Rotate in list order so nothing is forgotten for months.

<PullQuote cite="The only rule">
Stop at ten minutes, even if you are not finished. Unfinished is what makes tomorrow's ten minutes happen.
</PullQuote>

## What to buy (and what not to)

The cleaning industry sells complexity because complexity sells products. You need less than you think:

- A neutral pH multi-surface spray (or one bottle of washing-up liquid diluted)
- A microfibre cloth or two
- A squeegee or a glass cloth
- A bin bag, a bin liner, and somewhere to put them

You do not need a different product for every surface, a caddy, a colour-coded system, or an app. Those are purchases, not systems.

<Note title="On sharing the work">
If you live with other people, assign surfaces rather than tasks. "You own the sink" is enforceable; "we both clean the kitchen" is not. Two people doing four surfaces each takes five minutes, and nobody has to be the manager.
</Note>

## The first fortnight

For the first two weeks, the reset will feel slow and slightly pointless, because you are cleaning surfaces that are already mostly clean. That is the intended state — you are not cleaning a dirty house, you are preventing one.

By week three, the ten minutes will take seven, and the moment you walk in the door you will notice something you have not felt about your home in a while: it is fine. Nobody cleaned it. It was already fine, because yesterday you gave it ten minutes.
