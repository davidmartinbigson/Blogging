There is a genre of advice that says the answer to your phone is a screen-time limit, a greyscale filter, and a small amount of shame. It rarely works, and it is not your fault. Willpower is a terrible interface.

The approach that works is structural. You change the environment once, in a calm moment, so that the decision you would like to make is the default rather than a daily battle.

## 1. Turn off every notification except humans

This is the highest-return change on the list, and it takes about four minutes.

Go through your notification settings and keep only two categories: **messages from real people** and **anything genuinely time-critical** (a delivery, a flight, a bank alert). Turn off everything else: likes, follows, "trending", news alerts, promotional pushes, "we miss you" nudges.

<Note title="The test">
For each app, ask: if this stopped notifying me entirely, what would break? If the honest answer is "nothing", it is not a notification, it is advertising.
</Note>

Most phones let you do this in bulk: iOS has a "Scheduled Summary" that batches non-urgent notifications into two deliveries a day; Android has a notification channel per app. Both are worth ten minutes.

## 2. Remove the home screen

The placement of an icon is a far stronger signal than any intention. Move the apps you open reflexively off the first screen — into a folder, into the app library, or off the phone entirely.

A useful rule: **if you have opened an app more than twenty times this week without deciding to, it comes off the phone.** You can still use the website. The friction of typing an address is enough to convert a reflex into a decision.

## 3. Fix the accounts you have already lost

Security and attention are the same problem viewed from different angles. An account you cannot recover is an account that owns you.

- **Password manager first.** One manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, Apple Passwords, KeePassXC) and unique passwords everywhere. This single step removes most account-takeover risk.
- **Two-factor authentication, app-based.** Prefer an authenticator app or a hardware key over SMS, which is vulnerable to SIM-swap attacks. Start with email, banking and your main social account.
- **Check breach exposure.** Look up your address at Have I Been Pwned, then change the passwords for anything exposed and reuse nothing.
- **Recovery routes.** For your primary email account, confirm the recovery address and phone number are current. It is the master key to everything else.

## 4. Reduce what is tracked about you

You will not become anonymous, and chasing that is a distraction. The realistic goal is a smaller, tidier footprint.

| Setting | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Ad ID reset / limit tracking | Reduces cross-app profiling at almost no cost |
| Browser: block third-party cookies | Stops most cross-site tracking by default |
| Search: switch to a privacy-respecting engine | Cuts a large share of your search history trail |
| App permissions audit | Photo, location and microphone access are often unnecessary |
| Location: "while using" instead of "always" | Prevents background location logging |

Ten minutes in settings, twice a year, covers all of it.

## 5. Decide what you want the phone for

This is the part nobody writes about, because it is not a setting.

Make a short list: what do you actually want to do on this device? Reading, messaging three specific people, navigation, banking, a camera, music. Then notice how much of your usage falls outside the list.

The goal is not less phone. It is a phone that does the things you chose, and less of the things a growth team chose for you.

<Note title="A realistic experiment">
For one week, keep the phone out of the bedroom and charge it in another room overnight. No other changes. Most people report better sleep and a noticeably calmer morning. If that is the single change you make from this article, it is enough.
</Note>

## 6. Maintain it twice a year

Digital hygiene is not a one-off declutter; it is a small piece of maintenance, like replacing a smoke alarm battery. Put a recurring reminder in your calendar for January and July with this list:

1. Notifications audit (anything new and noisy?)
2. App deletion pass (what have you not opened in 30 days?)
3. Password manager check for reused or weak entries
4. Account permission review
5. Backups — phone, photos, documents — verified, not assumed

Twenty minutes, twice a year. That is the entire system, and it will do more for your attention and security than any app promising to fix either.
