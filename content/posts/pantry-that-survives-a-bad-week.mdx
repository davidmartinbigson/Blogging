There is a particular kind of bad week: you get home late, the fridge contains half a lemon and a suspicious jar, and the shops are a twenty-minute walk that feels like an hour. On those nights the difference between a decent dinner and an expensive takeaway is not skill. It is whether the cupboard has three things in it.

The list below is deliberately short. Ten items, chosen because they combine with each other in many directions and none of them spoils quickly.

## The ten

1. **Tinned chickpeas or white beans.** Protein, texture, and a cooking liquid that thickens things.
2. **Tinned tomatoes.** The base of half the pantry meals you will ever cook.
3. **Dried pasta.** The fastest complete meal in the cupboard.
4. **Rice.** Cheap, filling, and pairs with everything else on this list.
5. **Olive oil.** Carries flavour; the one item worth spending more on.
6. **Garlic.** The cheapest way to make plain food taste liked.
7. **Onions.** The foundation of almost everything savoury.
8. **A hard cheese.** Parmesan, pecorino, or a firm cheddar. Intensity per gram is extraordinary.
9. **Tinned fish.** Sardines, mackerel, tuna. A whole meal with no cooking.
10. **Stock cubes or paste.** Turns water into something that tastes like a decision was made.

<Note title="Salt, fat, acid, heat">
Add salt, black pepper, a vinegar or lemon, and chilli flakes as permanent residents. They are not ingredients, they are tools — and without them the ten items above taste like ingredients.
</Note>

## Why these ten and not thirty

Most pantry lists are really shopping lists. They assume a large kitchen, a car, and a fantasy version of yourself who dry-fries spices on a Tuesday.

This list is a **minimum viable pantry**: the smallest set that produces a real meal with nothing else in the house.

- It fits in two cupboard shelves.
- It costs roughly $45–60 to establish from empty.
- Every item is shelf-stable for months.
- Each one appears in at least four of the meals below.

## Eight meals from those ten items

No recipe required — these are methods, and the quantities are forgiving.

**1. Pasta with garlic and beans.** Sizzle sliced garlic in olive oil, add a drained tin of chickpeas, crush a third of them, toss with pasta and its water, finish with cheese and pepper.

**2. Tomato and fish pasta.** Tinned tomatoes, a tin of sardines broken up, chilli, garlic, fifteen minutes. The sardines dissolve and taste like the sea rather than like fish.

**3. Rice and beans, properly.** Onion and garlic until soft, a tin of tomatoes, a drained tin of beans, rice, stock, lid on. Twenty minutes. Eat it with cheese on top.

**4. Crispy chickpeas on anything.** Dry-fry a tin of chickpeas in a little oil until they rattle, salt hard, add chilli and a squeeze of lemon. Twenty minutes of hunger, solved.

**5. Tomato rice.** Same as a risotto, without the stirring: onion, garlic, tomato, rice, stock, lid on, rest for ten minutes off the heat.

**6. Pasta with nothing but cheese and pepper.** Cacio e pepe, if you are careful with the water; a very good cheese-and-butter pasta if you are not. Ten minutes.

**7. Tinned fish on toast.** With onion sliced thin, a lot of lemon, and olive oil. This is a proper dinner, and it is five minutes of work.

**8. The end-of-week soup.** Whatever is left, plus a stock cube, salt, and a piece of bread. Not exciting; reliably good.

## The part nobody mentions: rotation

A pantry fails through neglect rather than shortage. The cheap way to prevent that is a **use-first shelf** at eye level, where anything approaching its date lives, and a rule that new purchases go behind the old ones.

Two habits keep it honest:

- **Cook from the pantry at least once a week**, even in a good week. Pantry meals are a skill, and skills need repetition.
- **Date anything you freeze.** A freezer full of unlabelled containers is a cupboard with extra steps.

## What to add once the ten are established

Once the base is working, the highest-value additions are the ones that expand combinations, not the ones that add single dishes:

- **Dried lentils** — six meals for the price of one.
- **A vinegar you like** — sherry or red wine.
- **Frozen peas** — the best vegetable-per-cube-of-freezer in existence.
- **Tinned coconut milk** — turns the same base into a different cuisine.
- **Cumin and smoked paprika** — the shortest route from "food" to "flavour".

None of these need to be bought in the same month. The list works on its own; everything else is an upgrade.

## The actual point

A pantry is not about being organised or frugal in the way that magazines mean. It is about capacity. On the nights when the week has gone badly, you want the number of decisions between you and dinner to be close to zero.

Ten items on a shelf is a small thing. It is also the difference between a bad week and a bad month.
