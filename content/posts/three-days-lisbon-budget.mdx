Lisbon rewards walking and punishes planning too tightly. The city is built on seven hills, which means every route is either a climb or a descent, and the best moments tend to happen between the things you scheduled.

This itinerary assumes three full days, a mid-range budget, and a preference for eating well over seeing everything.

## Before you arrive

- **Accommodation neighbourhood.** Baixa and Chiado are central and flat but pricier. Alfama is atmospheric and steep. Príncipe Real is quieter, stylish and walkable to most of the city. Avoid staying far out to save €20 a night; you will spend it on transport and lose an hour a day.
- **Transit card.** Buy a rechargeable Viva Viagem card at any metro station (€0.50 for the card) and load it as you go. A single journey is around €1.85; a 24-hour pass is worth it only on days with four or more trips.
- **Shoes.** Genuinely: cobblestones plus hills. This is not the trip for new leather soles.
- **Book ahead.** The Jerónimos Monastery, a fado dinner, and — in summer — anything near Belém.

<Note title="What three days actually cost">
A comfortable mid-range budget is roughly €120–160 per person per day: €70–110 for a private room, €25–40 for food, €10–15 for transport and admissions, and the rest for coffee, wine and the occasional taxi up a hill. Cut the room to a hostel private and you land nearer €80–100.
</Note>

## Day one: Alfama, the castle, and a viewpoint at golden hour

Start high and walk down, not the other way around. Lisbon's hills are far kinder in one direction.

**Morning — Castelo de São Jorge.** Arrive at opening (9 a.m.) to avoid the cruise-ship wave. The castle itself is modest; the terrace view over the river and the red roofs is the point. Twenty minutes of peacocks and tiles, then leave.

**Late morning — Alfama on foot.** Wander down through the narrow streets towards the river: Largo das Portas do Sol, the church of Santa Engrácia (the National Pantheon), and the tiny squares where laundry hangs across the street. This is the neighbourhood that survived the 1755 earthquake, and it feels like it.

**Lunch — a tasca, not a view.** Pick a small tasca with plastic chairs and a menu in Portuguese. Look for *prato do dia*: a daily plate of grilled fish, rice and salad for €8–12.

**Afternoon — the riverfront.** Walk from Santa Apolónia towards Praça do Comércio along the water. Stop at the Time Out Market only if you want a single convenient meal; the food is good and the prices are 40% higher than a neighbourhood tasca.

**Evening — fado in Alfama.** A small fado house with a fixed menu runs €35–55 per person including wine. Book one that seats fewer than forty people; the amplification in larger venues removes the thing you came for.

## Day two: Belém in the morning, Bairro Alto at night

**Morning — Belém.** Take tram 15 from Praça da Figueira (or the train from Cais do Sodré to Belém, which is faster and less crowded). Go straight to the **Pastéis de Belém** for a warm custard tart — the queue moves quickly and the sit-down room at the back is worth the extra euro.

Then the **Jerónimos Monastery** (book a timed ticket) and, if the line is short, the **Belém Tower**. If both queues are long, choose the monastery; the tower's interior is far smaller than its postcard suggests.

**Afternoon — the MAAT and the river.** The Museum of Art, Architecture and Technology sits on the water and has a roof you can walk on. Even if you skip the exhibitions, the building and the riverside path are free and excellent.

**Evening — Bairro Alto.** This is the neighbourhood that invented the Lisbon evening: people drink cheaply on the street from 8 p.m., then eat late. A glass of wine at a hole-in-the-wall bar is €3–5. Dinner after 21:30, and expect to be out past midnight.

<PullQuote>
Lisbon does not have a nightlife district so much as a nightly migration: Bairro Alto for standing outside with a plastic cup, Cais do Sodré for dancing, and a taxi home at 3 a.m. that costs less than your last two drinks.
</PullQuote>

## Day three: markets, miradouros and a slow finish

**Morning — Campo de Ourique market.** A neighbourhood market with proper produce and a food hall. Fewer tourists than Time Out, better coffee, and a genuine sense of a city going about its Saturday.

**Midday — Príncipe Real.** Independent shops, the Jardim do Príncipe Real with its enormous cedar, and lunch at a café facing the park.

**Afternoon — the miradouro crawl.** Lisbon's viewpoints are free and they are the city's best attraction. A short route: **Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara** (facing the castle), **Miradouro da Graça** (locals, kiosk, sunset), and **Senhora do Monte** (the highest, usually quiet). Time it so you end at Graça for the last hour of light.

**Last dinner — seafood, properly.** Look for a marisqueira away from the main squares. Grilled sardines in season, clams in garlic and coriander (*amêijoas à Bulhão Pato*), and a bottle of vinho verde. Expect €25–35 per person with wine.

<Note title="If you have a fourth day">
Go to Sintra — but go early and go on a weekday. Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira are both worth it; the Moorish Castle is a fine view but a crowded one. The train from Rossio takes 40 minutes and costs under €5 return.
</Note>

## Eating well without trying too hard

- **Lunch is the main meal.** Portuguese restaurants serve generous *prato do dia* at lunch for half the evening price.
- **Couvert is charged.** Bread, olives and butter that appear unrequested are billable (€2–4). You can decline them.
- **Coffee is cheap.** An espresso is €0.80–1.20 at a counter. Drinking it standing is the local method.
- **Tipping is modest.** Round up, or leave 5% for good service.
- **Water.** Ask for *água da torneira* if you do not want to pay for a bottle; the tap water is safe.

## What to skip

The **Elevador de Santa Justa** queue (view is comparable to free miradouros), the **Time Out Market** as a dinner destination (fine for a snack, expensive for a meal), and any restaurant with photographs of the food and a man outside inviting you in. In a city this good at food, those three shortcuts are always a mistake.
